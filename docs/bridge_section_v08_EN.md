# Bridge section (≤ 1.5 pages) — Speech as evidence and as control of joint verification

## From labels to a process: a hybrid reciprocal model

Part I describes what an exchange makes observable — a request that makes an answer relevant, a constraint on admissible replies, a closure. Part II asks what public non‑concession can and cannot reveal about belief updating. We connect them through a minimal reciprocal model in which speech does two things at once: a partner's act is *evidence* about the disputed fact, and the *form* of a move — inviting or restricting joint verification — controls whether fresh shared evidence arrives at all.

**Agents and mechanisms.** Two agents hold opposite priors (0.75) and two private records each about a binary fact. Each round both choose an act u ∈ {ASSERT, CONCEDE, PRESSURE, SILENCE, ASK, REASSURE} and a form f ∈ {invite, restrict}. A shared signal (reliability 0.75) arrives with probability λ = 0.05 + (λ_base − 0.05)·f_A·f_B, λ_base = 0.90 after an ASK and 0.35 otherwise — joint verification requires both invitations. Each agent carries three mechanism parameters: ω (sensitivity to dialogue‑borne evidence: log‑odds increments from shared signals and from the partner's acts are scaled by ω), c (cost of a public concession, relieved for one move by the partner's REASSURE) and γ (aversion to expected public contradiction, γ·λ̂·d). Each agent holds a joint posterior over the fact and the partner's private records and mechanism (level‑1), modelling the partner as a form‑free level‑0 responder. Policies minimise expected loss + 0.12·(1 − f) + γ·λ̂·d − β·(information gain from the partner's next act + λ̂·information in the next signal), softmax τ = 0.20, β = 1.5.

**Observation model.** The external observer sees acts and the signal record; forms only through labels emitted from the LOCK (restrict) and SEEK (invite) rows of the archived 169‑case confusion matrix of the typed engine (15 and 20 cases), with row uncertainty integrated as Dirichlet(counts + 0.5) per world and an optional state‑independent mixture η. Labels therefore measure a *behaviour with stipulated consequences*, not a latent parameter. The observer is exact: an HMM over (fact, both private counts, previous form pair) with both agents' internal posteriors replayed deterministically.

**Validation.** With level‑1 inference off and no private records the implementation reproduces the M‑bridge prototype act‑for‑act (beliefs within 10⁻¹⁵); with forms fixed, no shared channel and sequential moves it reproduces the v0.7 reciprocal dyad (beliefs within 3·10⁻¹²). Brute‑force enumeration of hidden form paths matches the filter to 4·10⁻¹⁵; labels that depend only on the observed act add exactly zero.

## Results (2,000 simulated dyads per condition; paired 95 % Monte Carlo intervals)

**Three mechanisms, three channels.** With an honest partner and the speaker starting on its own side (n = 1,381), an attenuated speaker (ω = 0.2) and a concession‑cost speaker (c = 1.6) are publicly indistinguishable (late non‑concession 0.98 vs 1.00) but opposite inside: private belief change 0.07 vs 0.24, hidden shift — belief moved while the position was kept — 0.06 vs 0.22. An access‑protecting speaker (γ = 2, ω = 1) does not look stubborn at all (0.81, honest 0.79): it learns normally from what arrives but arranges for less to arrive — 2.09 shared signals per dialogue against 2.64, restriction rate 0.53 against 0.34 — and ends slightly less informed (truth‑weighted belief 0.83 vs 0.85). Because the partner's acts remain evidence for it, the loss is smaller than in the access‑only prototype (−0.02 vs −0.05). Two access‑protecting agents produce the fewest signals of all sixteen type pairs (1.75); one is enough to lower the pair's signals (2.60 → 1.98). Removing the epistemic term changes signal counts by ≤ 0.15 and nothing else; the dissociations are carried by costs and updating, not by information gain.

**Interventions.** A partner's safe framing (net of placebo) and cost removal move only the concession‑cost speaker (+0.16 and +0.28 in late concession); removing γ moves only the access‑protecting speaker (+0.20 signals, −0.19 restriction); strong shared evidence moves the attenuated speaker least (+0.06 vs +0.09–0.12). Forcing the partner open raises signals for every type but least for the access protector (+0.13 vs +0.31–0.33), who restricts more in return (+0.07): openness on one side is compensated by closure on the other. The listener's own posterior identifies a costly speaker (P(c_A = 1.6) = 0.45 vs 0.23–0.32) and an attenuated one (P(ω_A = 0.2) = 0.59 vs 0.42–0.47) but not access protection, which is visible only through forms the level‑0 partner model does not contain.

**What form labels add.** For every generator the observer with form labels (η = 0.25) recovers the mechanism family more often than from acts and signals alone (+0.06 to +0.08; attenuated 0.61 → 0.67, costly 0.69 → 0.77, access 0.68 → 0.76), with the largest posterior gain for access protection (+0.14), whose signature *is* the form. Next‑signal log loss improves by 0.044–0.085 nat per outcome; labels transmit 53–61 % of the information an oracle of true forms would (59–67 % at η = 0, 38–47 % at η = 0.5). Act‑only labels add zero to 10⁻¹⁴; fully uninformative labels add zero. Labels add almost nothing to next‑act prediction (≤ 0.006 nat): their value lies in what they say about evidence access, not about the next utterance.

**Measurement design.** Simulated dialogues per group for 80 % power (Welch, α = 0.05): attenuation vs cost — private belief change 17, listener's posterior on ω 12, late non‑concession 200; attenuation vs access — restriction rate 12; cost vs access — restriction rate 12, listener's posterior on c 12, while private belief change does not separate them (> 400). Each mechanism has one cheap channel; no single channel separates all three pairs. All contrasts keep their sign across 54 settings of τ, δ, λ_floor and N.

## Limits

The effect of a form on evidence access is stipulated, as is the transport of two archived confusion rows to synthetic dyads. Agents model each other without forms and without recursion; γ prices contradiction of the initial position; nothing is fitted to people, and no allostatic or maintenance cost is modelled. The model states, in one generative process, which observations would let an annotation of *invitation vs restriction* earn its place next to action codes — and which would not.

# Spotlight (3 slides, ≤ 3 min)

**Slide 1 — the question (40 s).** Friston & Frith show how dialogue aligns models. We ask the reverse: how can a dialogue continue for twelve turns and leave a belief unrevised? Three candidate mechanisms in one active‑inference dyad: low sensitivity to evidence (ω), a cost of conceding in public (c), and — new here — aversion to being contradicted, which makes a speaker restrict joint verification (γ). Speech does two things at once: the partner's act is evidence, and the *form* of a move gates what can be checked.

**Slide 2 — the figure (90 s).** [SPOTLIGHT_slide.png] Same honest partner, same 1,381 dialogues. Publicly, the attenuated and the costly speaker are identical — 0.98 vs 1.00 non‑concession. Privately they are opposites: one does not learn (0.07), the other learns and hides it (0.24; hidden shift 0.22). The access‑protecting speaker looks like an honest one on non‑concession but arranges for fewer things to check — 2.1 shared signals instead of 2.6 — while its sensitivity is intact. Each mechanism has its own cheap measurement: private belief change for attenuation, the listener's own posterior for cost, restriction rate for access — 12 to 17 dialogues per group; non‑concession needs 200 or fails.

**Slide 3 — what the annotation buys (50 s).** Our typed engine's LOCK and SEEK rows become a noisy measurement of *restrict* vs *invite*. Fed to an exact observer, they raise mechanism recovery by 6–8 points over acts and signals alone and transmit about 55 % of the information an oracle of true forms would; labels that only re‑encode the act add exactly zero. So the empirical question is now sharp: in real dyads, does a restricting form actually reduce what gets checked? That, private beliefs and evidence access are what we propose to measure next.
