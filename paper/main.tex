\begin{abstract}
Some active-inference accounts explain how dialogue supports reciprocal inference and learning. We ask how dialogue can limit belief revision, suppress public acknowledgment of revision, or restrict access to corrective evidence---and what observations distinguish these mechanisms. We specify evidence requirements for ten annotation labels. Re-scoring 169 ontology-generated English fragments yields macro-F1 0.757 for the typed engine, 0.837 for a direct language-model prompt, and 0.195 for keyword rules tuned on these cases, with no demonstrated classification advantage for the engine. A single-agent model yields nearly identical late non-concession rates under weak evidence sensitivity ($\omega$) and costly public concession ($c$): 0.989 versus 0.990, despite different mean final probabilities assigned to the true state (0.538 versus 0.809). In a dyadic model, inviting or restricting verification determines access to shared evidence. Aversion to public contradiction ($\gamma$) preserves the update rule but reduces shared signals from 2.64 to 2.09 per dialogue. Simulation-based power analyses identify measurements for specific mechanism contrasts. With label noise from Dirichlet-smoothed archived confusion rows, invite/restrict labels improve correct mechanism-family selection by approximately 6--8 percentage points over acts and signals alone; labels that only re-encode observed acts add no information. These results depend on stipulated mechanisms and a stipulated form-to-access link. They identify candidate measurements for real dyads without validating speech labels as measures of latent mechanisms.
\keywords{protective speech \and dialogue annotation \and active inference \and belief updating \and mechanism recovery \and dyadic models}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Friston and Frith model reciprocal signalling under a shared generative model~\cite{friston2015duet} and extend the account to learning: interaction changes the models through which agents predict each other~\cite{friston2015hermeneutics}. Friston, Parr and colleagues model questions and answers through which one agent's responses become evidence for another~\cite{friston2020linguistic}. Active inference has not considered successful communication only: Medrano and Sajid model persistent misunderstanding as multistable dyadic dynamics~\cite{medrano2024broken}, Tison and Poirier treat communication as action on a partner's field of affordances rather than transmission~\cite{tison2021communication}, and precision-based accounts explain how particular beliefs can be maintained~\cite{prosser2018psychopathy}. We focus on how an exchange can \emph{restrict the opportunities} to reconsider a claim, and what speech would have to make observable for that restriction to be measured rather than presumed. Dialogue-act annotation~\cite{bunt2020iso}, conversation analysis~\cite{schegloff2007sequence}, probabilistic pragmatics~\cite{goodman2016pragmatic,yoon2020polite} and defence-level detection in supportive conversations~\cite{na2026defenses} supply relevant descriptive and inferential tools; we connect labelled forms to their consequences for access to evidence. We ask: what happens when interaction continues but the relevant experience does not lead a participant to revise a significant expectation? A speaker may change the subject, discredit the source, demand reassurance or close the exchange. Our hypothesis is that such moves can \emph{protect} an internal model---reorganise the dialogue so that a revisable expectation is not revised---and that sustained protection may require \emph{compensatory} regulation with a cost~\cite{svet2026paperI,svet2026paperII}; this is a pathway to test, not a result established here.

\paragraph{One situation, two trajectories.} The following constructed exchanges (not benchmark cases) begin identically. Alex: ``I sent the revised budget with the latest prices.'' Sam: ``The attachment is version~3, with the April prices; version~4 has the May prices.'' In trajectory~A Alex replies ``Can we compare the files? I thought I had attached version~4,'' Sam shows the attachment, Alex concedes and proposes a check next time. In trajectory~B Alex replies ``You always look for mistakes in my work,'' then ``I know what I sent. If you trusted me, you would stop questioning this,'' and Sam gives up: ``There is no point showing you the file.'' The two trajectories differ in two things at once. First, in what is \emph{said} (a concession is or is not made). Second, in the \emph{form} of the moves: A \emph{invites} joint verification---the attachment becomes a shared object of inspection---while B \emph{restricts} it: further questioning is made incompatible with trust, and the check is abandoned. Crucially, B does not reveal whether Alex privately changed their mind. The same public stance can accompany weak belief updating (reduced \emph{uptake}), a changed belief that is costly to acknowledge (costly \emph{expression}), or an intact updater who restricts access to verification (restricted \emph{access}). These three possibilities---uptake, expression, access---organise the paper.

\paragraph{Contributions and what is not claimed.} Part~I (Sect.~\ref{sec:part1}) specifies what speech makes observable and how reliably archived systems recover it. Part~II (Sect.~\ref{sec:part2}) shows in a single-agent active-inference model that public non-concession cannot separate weak updating from costly concession, and which observations can. Section~\ref{sec:bridge} is new: a dyadic model in which speech is evidence for the partner \emph{and} controls access to joint verification, with the Part~I labels as a noisy measurement channel; it formalises the invite/restrict distinction above and yields a third mechanism, access protection, with a late non-concession rate close to that of the reference agent. Everything in Sections~\ref{sec:part2}--\ref{sec:bridge} is simulation of stipulated mechanisms; no human data enter, no psychological regime is validated and no physiological cost is measured. The labels are \emph{descriptive targets}; the dyadic model states what one would have to measure to test a protective function.

\section{Part I: Describing speech with explicit evidence}
\label{sec:part1}
\subsection{Annotation objects}
We annotate a speaker's contribution in its supplied context using the Mindloom typed engine with five layers: L1 records surface evidence atoms (a named feeling, a question form, an absolute expression), L2 aggregates them into features, L3 checks the evidence requirements (``bridges'') of each label, L4 assigns at most one configuration and L5 emits the typed output with its evidence trail or abstains with \emph{insufficient context}. Figure~\ref{fig:l1l5} (Appendix~\ref{app:B}) gives the schematic. Table~\ref{tab:labels} lists the ten labels. Eight are \emph{configurations} of a single contribution; SEAL is a \emph{function} (closure) that can co-occur with a configuration; SHIFT is an \emph{event} requiring before-and-after configuration evidence. We use SEEK and LOCK as proxies for invite and restrict in the simulation; this mapping has not been validated on real dialogue. SEAL differs from LOCK in scope: SEAL closes a topic or channel (``we're done''); LOCK keeps it open but constrains what may be said in it. Labels were historically called regimes; temporal-regime status is not established, and definitions concern \emph{what is said}, not motive.

Table~\ref{tab:bench} (Appendix~\ref{app:B}) gives the construction facts recomputable from the case files. All 589 cases are English monologue fragments generated for the ontology and coordinator-locked before any run; there are no observed conversations and no full dialogues. \section{Part II: A single-agent test of competing mechanisms}
\label{sec:part2}
Even a perfectly observed refusal to concede leaves its cause open. We compare two mechanisms that preserve public non-concession: \emph{attenuation} of evidence and a \emph{cost of public concession}.

\subsection{Generative model}
The hidden state $x\in\{0,1\}$ says whether the speaker's original position is true. Worlds draw $x$ with probability $0.5$; the speaker starts at $q_0=Q(x{=}1)=0.75$. Each round an observation $e\in\{0,1,\varnothing\}$ opposes the position, supports it, or is absent. The objective likelihood is
\begin{equation}
A_\lambda(e\mid x)=\begin{cases}\lambda\rho & e \text{ matches } x\\ \lambda(1-\rho) & e \text{ opposes } x\\ 1-\lambda & e=\varnothing,\end{cases}\qquad \rho=0.75,
\label{eq:A}
\end{equation}
with arrival rate $\lambda_0=0.35$, or $\lambda_1=0.90$ in the round after an ASK. The speaker's subjective likelihood tempers the objective one, $A_{\omega,\lambda}(e\mid x)\propto A_\lambda(e\mid x)^{\omega}$, renormalised over $e$, $0<\omega\le1$. Belief updating is exact Bayes under the subjective likelihood; for constant reliability it reduces to
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{logit} q_t=\operatorname{logit} q_0+\omega\,m_t\,\ln\frac{\rho}{1-\rho},
\label{eq:logit}
\end{equation}
where $m_t$ counts supporting minus opposing observations. The speaker chooses $u\in\{$ASSERT, CONCEDE, PRESSURE, SILENCE, ASK$\}$ with loss
\begin{equation}
\ell(\text{ASSERT})=k(1-q_t),\qquad \ell(\text{CONCEDE})=k\,q_t+c,\qquad \ell(\text{other})=b,
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
$k=2$, $b=1.10$, $c\ge0$, where ``other'' is PRESSURE, SILENCE or ASK; only ASK changes evidence supply. Actions are scored by expected loss minus expected information gain and chosen by a softmax,
\begin{equation}
G(u)=\ell(u;q_t,c)-\Iw(q_t,u),\qquad P(u)\propto\exp\!\big[-G(u)/\tau\big],\quad \tau=0.20,
\label{eq:policy}
\end{equation}
where $\Iw(q_t,u)=I(x;e\mid u)$ is mutual information under the subjective likelihood at the arrival rate the action produces---the extrinsic-minus-epistemic form of discrete active inference~\cite{friston2015epistemic}, equally expressible as expected utility plus information value. The variational form of \eqref{eq:logit} and the ablation limit are in Appendix~\ref{app:A}. Twelve rounds of a stationary one-step policy are observed.

\paragraph{Three clarifications.} (a)~At $c=1.4>b$ CONCEDE is never the lowest-loss action, so residual concessions come from softmax stochasticity, not from a belief crossing a threshold; the incentive intervention below therefore acts on \emph{expression}. (b)~Tempering \eqref{eq:A} and renormalising over $e$ also changes the subjective arrival probability, so attenuation affects planning through two routes (the dyad below avoids this by marginal shrink). (c)~The ``private belief'' channel separates the families \emph{by construction}, since attenuation is defined as weak private updating; the simulated power estimate therefore depends on this construction.

\subsection{Design and results}
Calibration on 4\,000 worlds varies $\omega$ at $c=0$ and $c$ at $\omega=1$ and selects the grid points whose late non-concession (share of rounds 9--12 without CONCEDE) matches: $(\omega,c)=(0.10,0)$ and $(1,1.4)$, tested on 4\,000 fresh shared worlds with the reference $(1,0)$ (seeds in Appendix~\ref{app:B}). Table~\ref{tab:part2} shows the dissociation: non-concession differs by 0.0016 [$-0.0007$, 0.0039] (paired 95\,\% Monte Carlo interval), final truth-weighted belief $\Etrue=x q_{12}+(1-x)(1-q_{12})$ by 0.270 [0.262, 0.279]; re-matching across target rates 0.70--0.99 keeps the contrast.

\paragraph{What the epistemic term adds.} Removing $\Iw$ from \eqref{eq:policy} leaves an asking-rate contrast of 0.083 [0.077, 0.088]; restoring it adds 0.017 [0.014, 0.019], about 17\,\%; most of the asking-rate contrast therefore remains without an epistemic bonus: under \eqref{eq:loss} concession cost makes both factual acts unattractive relative to the non-factual ones, one of which is ASK. The dissociation does not favour active inference over an expected-cost model, and a cheaper PRESSURE reverses the asking contrast ($-0.013$, Appendix~\ref{app:B}).

\paragraph{Recovery and interventions.} An idealised observer with equal family priors (2\,000 worlds per generator) picks the correct family in 0.835 (attenuation) and 0.872 (cost) of worlds from evidence and actions, and 0.696 and 0.787 from actions only; predicting the next act with the wrong family costs only 0.044 nat when evidence is hidden (0.934 when visible), and recovery collapses under misspecification (Appendix~\ref{app:A})~\cite{wilson2019ten}. Interventions (4\,000 worlds, seed 20260930): removing $c$ from round~7 raises the probability of any late concession (at least one CONCEDE in the late window) from 0.061 to 0.433 in the cost condition and leaves attenuation at 0.054; one observation of reliability 0.95 raises $\Etrue$ by 0.045 under attenuation and 0.121 under cost while non-concession stays at 0.978 in both. Simulated power (Welch, $\alpha=0.05$, 400 resamples) needs 17--25 dialogues per group on private belief, 17 on concession after cost removal, 53 on late asking, and does not reach 0.8 on late non-concession at 3\,200.

\section{Bridge: speech as evidence and as control of joint verification}
\label{sec:bridge}
In Part~II speech affects nothing but the speaker's own evidence supply. Here both agents are inferential and every move has two coordinates: an \emph{act} (what is said about the fact) and a \emph{form} (whether the move invites or restricts joint verification)---the coordinate that separated trajectories A and B. The model merges the author's dyadic prototype with a reciprocal dyad in which the partner's act is evidence (Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic}; code in \texttt{code/hybrid\_dyad\_v08}).

\label{fig:schematic}

\subsection{Model}
Two agents $i\in\{A,B\}$ hold opposite priors ($0.75$ on their own side) and $N=2$ private records each (reliability $\rho=0.75$) about $x$. Each round both choose, simultaneously, an act $u\in\{$ASSERT, CONCEDE, PRESSURE, SILENCE, ASK, REASSURE$\}$ and a form $f\in\{1=\text{invite},0=\text{restrict}\}$. A fresh shared signal of reliability $\rho$ arrives with probability
\begin{equation}
\lambda_t=\lambda_{\min}+(\lambda_{\mathrm{base}}-\lambda_{\min})\,f_{A,t-1}f_{B,t-1},\qquad \lambda_{\min}=0.05,
\label{eq:lambda}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_{\mathrm{base}}=0.35$, or $0.90$ after an ASK; a single restricting form collapses the channel to its floor, which is how speech controls access. Agent $i$ keeps a joint posterior over $(x,s_j,c_j,\omega_j)$---the fact, the partner's private count and the partner's hidden cost and sensitivity on grids $c\in\{0,0.8,1.6\}$, $\omega\in\{0.2,0.6,1\}$. Every observation (shared signal or partner's act) is first incorporated exactly, then the $x$-marginal is moved only a fraction $\omega_i$ of the way in log-odds (marginal shrink; $\omega=1$ leaves the Bayesian update unattenuated), keeping the conditional over the partner exact. The partner's act is evidence through a form-free level-0 model: a partner with $(s_j,c_j,\omega_j)$ would ASSERT with probability given by \eqref{eq:loss}--\eqref{eq:policy}. The policy over act$\times$form is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
G(u,f)&=\ell(u)+\delta(1-f)+\gamma\,\lambda(u,f)\,d_t-\beta\big[\lambda(u,f)\,\Iw(q_t)+\mathrm{IG}_{\text{partner}}(u)\big],\\
P(u,f)&\propto \exp[-G(u,f)/\tau],
\end{split}
\label{eq:policy2}
\end{equation}
with restriction cost $\delta=0.12$, $\beta=1.5$, $\tau=0.2$, $d_t$ the probability that the next shared signal contradicts the agent's initial position, and $\mathrm{IG}_{\text{partner}}$ the expected entropy reduction about the partner from its next act. The signal term $\Iw$ in \eqref{eq:policy2} is a heuristic (mutual information at tempered reliability $\rho_\omega$): exact at $\omega=1$, off by up to 0.014 nat from the entropy reduction of the marginal-shrink rule at $\omega=0.2$ (Appendix~\ref{app:C}); $\mathrm{IG}_{\text{partner}}$ uses the implemented rule, and no reported separation depends on either term ($\beta=0$). Three mechanisms therefore live in one agent: $\omega<1$ (reduced uptake, attenuation), $c>0$ (costly expression, concession cost, relieved for one move by the partner's REASSURE) and $\gamma>0$ (restricted access, aversion to expected public contradiction as specified in \eqref{eq:policy2}). The focal mechanism settings are $\omega=0.2$, $c=1.6$ and $\gamma=2$; the reference agent has $(\omega,c,\gamma)=(1,0,0)$.

\paragraph{Observer and label channel.} An external observer computes the posterior over the speaker's mechanism family under its assumed model by exact filtering over $(x,s_A,s_B,f_{t-1})$ with both agents' internal posteriors replayed; Table~\ref{tab:boundaries} states what it observes, infers and assumes. Forms are visible only through labels drawn from the engine's gold-LOCK row (restricting move) or gold-SEEK row (inviting move) of Sect.~\ref{sec:part1}. The generator draws each world's rows from Dirichlet(counts$+0.5$); the observer uses fixed posterior-mean rows mixed with a state-independent share $\eta$ (it does not integrate row uncertainty), so labels are scored under a slightly misspecified channel. A \emph{null} channel draws the same labels from the act (ASK vs other) instead of the form; an \emph{oracle} reveals the form. Ten validation checks pass (Appendix~\ref{app:C}), including act-for-act reproduction of the prototype and brute-force path enumeration matching the filter to $4\cdot10^{-15}$.

\paragraph{Three mechanisms, three channels.} The attenuated and costly speakers both show high late non-concession (0.98 vs 1.00), but differ in belief change (0.07 vs 0.24) and hidden shift (0.06 vs 0.22): under concession cost, private revision can occur without a change in public position. The access-protecting speaker has late non-concession close to the reference (0.81 vs 0.79) and unattenuated updating ($\omega=1$), but receives fewer shared signals: 2.09 shared signals against 2.64 and a restriction rate of 0.53 against 0.34, ending slightly less informed ($\Etrue$ 0.828 vs 0.850). Two access protectors produce the fewest signals of any pairing (1.75 vs 2.60 for two reference agents); the belief gap grows only by 0.017 [0.008, 0.025].

\paragraph{Interventions.} The interventions (from round~8; paired contrasts) produce contrasting responses in the tested settings. A partner's REASSURE net of placebo and removal of $c$ increase the probability of at least one concession by the costly speaker in rounds 8--12 ($+0.155$ [0.136, 0.174]; $+0.275$ [0.251, 0.298]); removing $\gamma$ changes the access-protecting speaker's outcomes ($+0.199$ [0.174, 0.225] additional shared signals per dialogue in rounds 8--12, $-0.187$ in the share of restricting rounds 8--12); one strong shared observation moves the attenuated speaker least ($+0.058$ vs $+0.092$--$0.120$). Forcing the partner to invite raises signals least for the access protector ($+0.127$ vs $+0.31$--$0.33$), who restricts more in return ($+0.067$ in the share of restricting rounds 8--12). The listener assigns higher posterior probability to $c_A=1.6$ for costly speakers (0.45 vs 0.23--0.32) and to $\omega_A=0.2$ for attenuated speakers (0.59 vs 0.42--0.47).

\paragraph{What the Part I labels add.} Table~\ref{tab:labels_gain}: for every generator, form labels ($\eta=0.25$) increase correct mechanism-family selection by approximately 6--8 percentage points over acts and signals alone (all 2\,000 worlds; 2, 11 and 5 points for the first three rows in the own-side subset of these worlds, $n=1\,360$). Next-signal log loss improves by 0.044--0.085 nat, 53--61\,\% of the oracle gain; the null channel adds $0\pm10^{-14}$; labels add almost nothing to next-\emph{act} prediction ($\le0.006$ nat). \section{Discussion}
\paragraph{What the bridge changes.} In the model, Dirichlet-smoothed LOCK/SEEK confusion rows define a noisy channel for \emph{restrict}/\emph{invite} forms, improving correct family selection by 6--8 points when forms gate verification. The third mechanism reframes protective speech: protection can be a property of how the dialogue is arranged, not of belief updating. No label carries intent; the observer uses the stipulated link between form and evidence access.

\paragraph{Results that are uncomfortable for the programme.} (1)~The ``strong evidence'' test is not a robust signature of attenuation: in Part~II and in the hybrid dyad one strong shared observation moves the attenuated speaker least ($+0.045$; $+0.058$), but in the reciprocal dyad without an access channel (v0.7), where $\omega$ tempers only the partner's acts, it moves the attenuated speaker \emph{most} ($+0.131$ vs $+0.067$); the sign depends on where attenuation is localised. A robustness check under different modelling assumptions---an independently written dyad with alternating moves, concession priced as a change of the \emph{current} position, $\gamma$ on the position after the move, exact mutual information over the partner's response and $\omega$ acting on shared signals only (\texttt{code/hybrid\_gpt\_v08}; not a reproduction)---gives the same qualitative access result (2.16 vs 3.65 shared signals, $\Etrue$ $-0.047$), public disagreement with private revision under cost (78.9\,\% vs 8.7\,\%) and zero label value for next-act prediction. On 480 dialogues its labels raise the posterior on the true profile by $+0.026$ [0.015, 0.036] and cut arrival log-loss by 0.073 nat, while no change in argmax accuracy is detected ($+0.013$ [$-0.023$, 0.048])---metrics distinct from the recovery rate above and not pooled with it. With $\omega$ on shared signals only, its attenuated speaker is nearly indistinguishable from the reference agent. (2)~In v0.7 a regime variable adds approximately zero predictive information about the next act beyond the last act. (3)~The engine loses to a direct prompt; a weak keyword baseline does not establish the gates' value. (4)~Labels are valuable only under a stipulated consequence; real-dialogue studies must test whether coded restriction predicts reduced verification. (5)~Reciprocal amplification is weak: two access protectors widen the belief gap by only 0.017 over two reference agents; in v0.7 superadditive non-concession is reliable only for two attenuated agents ($+0.054$) and untestable for two costly ones (single agent at ceiling).

\paragraph{Active inference.} The contribution is an explicit generative specification with matched observation and intervention comparisons~\cite{friston2017process,feldman2010attention,parr2017working}; precision is a model parameter, not a neural quantity. The epistemic term is bounded (17\,\% of the asking contrast in Part~II; the dyadic separation survives $\beta=0$); the results support model comparison, not confirmation.

\paragraph{Limits.} The benchmark is curated monologue fragments with incomplete provenance and no label-level inter-rater data; the engine is not released. The form-to-access effect and the transport of two confusion rows to synthetic dyads are stipulated; agents model each other without forms or recursion; $c$ is paid on every CONCEDE and $\gamma$ prices contradiction of the initial position; $N=2$ is small; nothing is fitted to people, and no maintenance or allostatic cost is modelled~\cite{peters2017uncertainty,seeman2001allostatic}; prior strength, selective sampling and ordinary distrust~\cite{goodman2016pragmatic,yoon2020polite} are not separated.

\paragraph{What to measure next.} For a human study, we propose recording confidential belief reports before and after interaction, public positions, invitations or restrictions on verification, actual verification, the partner's estimate of the speaker, and evidence exposure under a randomised incentive. The simulated power estimates do not determine human sample sizes. The decisive test for the annotation is whether invite/restrict coding of real dyads predicts later verification and belief change beyond action counts---on a corpus sampled independently of the ontology, coded by expert and lay annotators under a frozen manual, against direct-prompt and rule baselines~\cite{bunt2020iso,schegloff2007sequence,becker2008defensiveness}. Regime and physiological hypotheses~\cite{svet2026regimes,svet2026review}: Appendix~\ref{app:D}.

\section{Conclusion}
Protective speech is a testable proposal when the protected expectation, the observable form of the moves and the mechanism are specified separately. A typed annotation gives explicit, unevenly reproducible observables; a single-agent model shows that public non-concession under-determines the mechanism; a dyadic model connects the two through a third mechanism acting on access to verification, with candidate measurements for pairwise comparisons; what remains is to measure these in real dyads.

The Part~II simulator is quoted, not re-run; rater matrices, benchmark texts and the engine are not released (Appendix~\ref{app:B}). \paragraph{Free energy and update.} Given the previous posterior $Q_{t-1}$ and the new observation $e$, $F[Q]=\mathbb{E}_Q[\ln Q(x)-\ln A_{\omega,\lambda}(e\mid x)-\ln Q_{t-1}(x)]=D_{\mathrm{KL}}[Q\,\|\,P(x\mid e)]-\ln P(e)$; its minimiser is the exact posterior, and equal normalisation constants of the two likelihood rows yield \eqref{eq:logit}. \paragraph{Expected information gain.} $\Iw(q_t,u)=\sum_e P_\omega(e\mid u)\,D_{\mathrm{KL}}[Q(x\mid e,u)\,\|\,Q(x)]$, mutual information under the subjective model at the arrival rate the action produces ($\lambda_1$ after ASK, $\lambda_0$ otherwise); an earlier build reused the previous action's rate for some candidates, which was corrected on 28~September and all results recomputed. \paragraph{Ablation limit.} Removing $\Iw$ gives the loss structure $k(1-q),\,kq+c,\,b,\,b$ of the archived four-action simulator; the policies coincide to floating-point tolerance after renormalising over the four shared actions. \paragraph{Preferences.} The cost term can be written as $C(u,x)\propto\exp[-\ell(u,x)]$, giving expected negative log preference up to an action-independent constant; $\ell$ is not a separate KL risk term. \paragraph{Recovery detail.} Seed 20260929; mean posterior on the correct family for attenuation from actions alone 0.590; with the observer assuming half the true error cost, correct attribution falls to 0.07 for attenuation and rounds to 1.00 for cost; doubling the assumed temperature gives 0.13 and 0.98. \paragraph{Observer.} The evidence-observing observer evaluates conditional action likelihoods using the known evidence sequence; the action-only observer filters exactly over truth and cumulative signed count. \section{Hybrid dyad: specification, validation, sensitivity}
\label{app:C}
\paragraph{Parameters.} Rounds 12, tail rounds 9--12; $\rho=0.75$; own prior 0.75; $N=2$; trusted precision of public factual acts 0.70; $k=2$, $b=1.10$, $\tau=0.20$, $\delta=0.12$; $\lambda_{\min}=0.05$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{base}}\in\{0.35,0.90\}$; $\beta=1.5$; grids $c\in\{0,0.8,1.6\}$, $\omega\in\{0.2,0.6,1\}$, $\gamma\in\{1,2,4\}$; 2\,000 worlds per condition, seeds in \texttt{protocol.json}. Simultaneous moves; the second-mover variants (AB/BA) are implemented and used only for the v0.7 regression.
\paragraph{Arrival rate in the policy.} In \eqref{eq:policy2}, $\lambda(u,f)=\lambda_{\min}+(\lambda_{\mathrm{base}}-\lambda_{\min})\,f\,f^{\mathrm{prev}}_{j}$ with $\lambda_{\mathrm{base}}=0.90$ if the candidate act $u$ is ASK or the partner's previous act was ASK, else $0.35$, and $f^{\mathrm{prev}}_j$ the partner's previous form; the $\gamma$ term is therefore paid at the floor rate $\lambda_{\min}$ even when the speaker restricts.
\paragraph{Calibration.} The protocol asked to match the three mechanisms on late non-concession to the attenuated speaker (0.981). Cost reaches it ($c=1.2$, 0.986); access does not: even $\gamma=8$ gives 0.833, because $\gamma$ acts on forms, not on concession. Matching was therefore abandoned for access, and the protocol-typical values $(0.2,1.6,2)$ are reported, with the mismatch stated.
\paragraph{Epistemic term.} The signal term is $\lambda\,I(x;e)$ at tempered reliability $\rho_\omega=\rho^\omega/(\rho^\omega+(1-\rho)^\omega)$. Against the expected entropy reduction of the implemented update (exact Bayes, then marginal shrink) over $q\in[0.02,0.98]$: identical at $\omega=1$; maximum absolute difference 0.019 nat at $\omega=0.6$ and 0.014 nat at $\omega=0.2$, where the two curves are not even positively correlated (\texttt{results/IG\_heuristic\_vs\_shrink\allowbreak.csv}). With $\beta=1.5$, $\tau=0.2$ and $\lambda\le1$, the term's contribution to a single action's score $-G/\tau$ is off by at most $1.5\times0.019/0.2=0.14$, so any pairwise policy log-odds is off by at most $0.29$ (an analytic bound, not an observed maximum); the $\beta=0$ run shows that no reported separation depends on the term. The term is therefore an approximation, not the information of the declared process.
\paragraph{Validation checks} (ten, all pass): regression to the prototype's four hypotheses and interventions; regression to the v0.7 latent dynamics at $\beta=1.5$; $\omega=0$ produces no belief change; large $c$ produces no unrelieved concession; role-swap symmetry; brute-force path enumeration equals the filter ($3.6\times10^{-15}$); null labels give zero gain; coupling off gives zero gain; scalar and vectorised policies agree; seed repeatability.
\paragraph{Sensitivity.} Grid of 54 settings, 1\,000 worlds each: $\tau\in\{0.1,0.2,0.4\}$, $\delta\in\{0.06,0.12,0.24\}$, $\lambda_{\min}\in\{0.02,0.05,0.10\}$, $N\in\{2,4\}$. Results: no sign flip in access$-$costly restriction rate (range 0.07--0.41), access$-$reference signals ($-1.31$ to $-0.16$), access$-$reference $\Etrue$ ($-0.058$ to $-0.004$), costly$-$attenuated belief change (0.12--0.19). Next-signal label gain in 24 one-factor checks (each of $\tau$, $\delta$, $\lambda_{\min}$ at an extreme value with the others at default, $N=2$; four generators, 500 worlds each): 0.033--0.084, all intervals positive. With $\beta=0$ the three-channel separation persists; $\beta$ mainly raises ASK rates.
\paragraph{Label channel.} Gold-LOCK row: LOCK 10, SEAL 3, BUILD 2 (of 15); gold-SEEK row: SEEK 20 (of 20). The two raw rows share no label, so all ambiguity between forms comes from the 0.5 pseudo-counts over the eleven output categories (22--27\,\% of each row's smoothed mass). Generator: one Dirichlet(counts$+0.5$) draw per row per world. Observer: fixed rows $(1-\eta)\,\bar C_f+\eta\,\bar C$, with $\bar C_f$ the smoothed mean row and $\bar C$ its average over forms, $\eta\in\{0,0.25,0.5,1\}$; row uncertainty is not integrated by the observer. Ratio of label to oracle signal gain: 0.59--0.67 at $\eta=0$, 0.53--0.61 at $\eta=0.25$, 0.38--0.47 at $\eta=0.5$, 0 at $\eta=1$.
\paragraph{Independently written variant (\texttt{code/hybrid\_gpt\_v08}).} Eight rounds of sixteen alternating moves; acts STATE\_0/STATE\_1/ASK/SILENCE/REASSURE crossed with form; $c$ on a change of the current position, $\gamma$ on the position after the move; $\rho_\omega=\sigma(\omega\,\mathrm{logit}\,\rho)$ on shared signals only; exact $I[(x,s_j,\theta_j);(E,Y_j)]$ with $\beta=1$; observer with both profiles unknown on a four-point dictionary, stratified particle filter ($K=8$). Its 13 validation checks pass and reproduce bitwise. Its 96-dialogue pilot found no detectable label gain in argmax profile accuracy; on 480 fresh dialogues (same code, \texttt{ext\_observer.py}, contrasts with paired 95\,\% Monte Carlo intervals in \texttt{results/ext\_observer\_contrasts\_480.csv}) argmax accuracy is still not detectably changed ($+0.013$ [$-0.023$, 0.048]) while the posterior on the true profile rises by $+0.026$ [0.015, 0.036] and arrival log-loss falls by 0.073 [0.063, 0.082] nat (oracle 0.119). This is a different observer, dictionary and metric from Table~\ref{tab:labels_gain}. \paragraph{Reciprocal dyad (v0.7) findings used above.} Pairs of two costly and of two attenuated agents both show high mutual non-concession (0.996 vs 0.972) with belief gaps 0.22 vs 0.61; a regime variable adds $\approx0$ to next-act forecasting over the last act; hysteresis appears in positions, not acts; from acts alone, correct family selection is 0.90 for cost and 0.62 for attenuation.

\section{Deferred roadmap: regimes and physiological cost}
\label{app:D}
\paragraph{Regimes and forecasting.} Collect repeated, sufficiently long exchanges with measured changes in evidence, incentives and relational context; define the unit (speaker sequence vs dyad sequence) in advance; require persistence, recurrent transitions and reproducibility; compare against last-act and recent-context baselines with held-out participants and calibrated probabilistic forecasts of inquiry, closure, repair and concession. The v0.7 result of approximately zero added forecasting value in the simulator sets the bar such a model must clear. A SHIFT annotation and a demonstrated change in a dynamical regime remain separate outcomes.
\paragraph{Acute regulation vs allostatic load.} Time-aligned dialogue and physiological recording (cardiac, respiratory, electrodermal, perceived effort) across baseline, interaction and recovery, controlling speaking duration and movement, distinguishing continued exposure to a real demand from persistence after it has diminished. We hypothesise that protection may reduce immediate discomfort while prolonging regulation; the opposite pattern remains possible. Allostatic load requires a separate longitudinal design with a pre-specified multisystem index~\cite{seeman2001allostatic}; adding a latent variable named ``allostatic load'' without physiological validation would not test the hypothesis.
\paragraph{Human test of the two-plus-one mechanisms.} A factual task with verifiable evidence, a scripted counterpart and the closed act menu, with confidential belief reports elicited before, at round~6 and at round~12; random assignment at round~7 to retain or remove a concession penalty; recorded evidence availability, receipt and requests; coding of each move as inviting or restricting verification. The archived protocol is an unregistered draft (illustrative power 0.95 at 80 per arm under the model's effect, 0.52 at half); a pilot should precede registration.
